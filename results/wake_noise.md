# Branch A under rotor noise (real takes + DREGON)

Generated by `python eval/wake_noise.py`. Do not edit by hand.

The 40 recorded takes of `results/wake_real_voice.md` (one speaker, 20 per class), mixed with DREGON hovering ego-noise from the **evaluation partition** (last 30% of the flight, 13.5 s; training augmentation used the first 70%) by `data/mix_noise.py`'s rule: SNR on the active-speech level, one excerpt per take reused at every SNR, seed 42. Noise also fills the 1 s pads either side. Scored as `eval/wake_real.py` scores the clean takes, at the deployed threshold **0.999**; the threshold was not tuned on these mixes. The take is the unit (n = 20 per class); intervals are exact (Clopper-Pearson).

## Read before quoting

- **NFR-16 is FRR <= 0.10**, judged on the whole interval. At n = 20 even zero misses leaves an upper bound of ~0.17, so no row here can meet it; a row whose lower bound exceeds 0.10 misses it.
- The `clean` row is this script's own run with no noise and must equal `results/wake_real_voice.md`; if it does not, the noisy rows are not comparable to it.
- Missed takes, with the peak their own head reached: clean: `swarm_hold`: w03 (0.9977); `swarm_abort`: none | 20 dB: `swarm_hold`: none; `swarm_abort`: none | 10 dB: `swarm_hold`: none; `swarm_abort`: w04 (0.9952) | 5 dB: `swarm_hold`: none; `swarm_abort`: none. Per-take peaks for both heads are in `results/wake_noise.json`.
- "Fired other class" counts takes on which the *other* head reached the threshold (a take can fire both). A hold heard as abort, or the reverse, is still a stop (prd.md SS4.2), so a confusion is fail-safe; a miss is not.
- **The noise-alone streams are short.** The eval partition is 13.5 s; with zero events the Poisson upper bound is several hundred per hour, far above NFR-15's 1/h. They can show that rotor noise by itself does or does not set the spotter off at a high rate, not that the criterion is met. The eight channels are eight microphones on one airframe at the same moments, not eight independent recordings.
- The 40 takes share one 13.5 s noise segment, so excerpts overlap between takes; the variation in noise across takes is narrower than a real flight's.
- 0 of 120 mixtures were attenuated for headroom (SNR unchanged).
- One speaker, one room, one flight recording; digital mixing holds the voice fixed, so this isolates noise, and it does not capture how the operator raises their voice over rotors (the Lombard effect).
